# Module 7 — 369th Parliamentary Standing Committee Report: Independent Oversight

## Source
`[PARLIAMENTARY OVERSIGHT]` Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports (Chair: Digvijaya Singh) — "Review of functioning and performance of Sports Authority of India (SAI) and Khelo India," presented 20 August 2025. Verified directly via PRS Legislative Research's official summary PDF (PRS is an independent, non-partisan legislative research body — not an SAI or Ministry self-description).

**Critical discipline, as agreed:** everything below is what the **Committee observed/recommended**, not confirmation that any of it has been acted upon. "The Committee recommended X" ≠ "X has happened." Where later evidence (NIPFP Jan 2026, AR 2023-24) confirms or updates a point, that's noted explicitly.

## Key observations and recommendations

| # | Observation | Recommendation | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SAI is the leading government sports-talent body but lacks statutory status | Department of Sports/SAI to examine converting SAI into a **statutory body with its own dedicated cadre** | Governance |
| 2 | SAI's financial allocation is very low; Akharas/IGMA academies get no regular financial assistance; sports-kit/nutrition stipends unrevised for years | Revise budgets; provide infrastructure financial support | Finance |
| 3 | Private bodies receive more CSR funding than government sports institutions; **NSDF funds have been diverted to civil services associations and residential colonies** | Revise CSR rules so government institutions can equally benefit; **stricter regulation of NSDF** | Finance/Vigilance-relevant |
| 4 | **~45% of SAI's sanctioned posts are vacant**, gaps covered by contract appointments | Fill vacancies within **6 months** | HR |
| 5 | India's medal strategy is diffuse compared to countries that focus on specific sports | Focus talent identification on India's highest-potential sports; target the 10–15 age group specifically | Policy/Operations |
| 6 | NSF recognition process is bureaucratic | Proactively recognise federations with medal potential (e.g., MMA, ice hockey named specifically) | Governance |
| 7 | Of 24 established NCOEs, some are non-operational, others lack residential facilities; distribution is geographically disproportionate to actual sporting culture | Establish NCOEs where sporting culture exists but SAI facilities don't; operationalise existing ones; **specifically operationalise IGMA schools in Jharkhand and Maharashtra** | Infrastructure/Operations |
| 8 | NCOEs don't integrate sports and education, creating an educational gap for athletes | All NCOEs/Regional Centres should become residential-cum-educational institutions from school through higher education | Policy/Operations |
| 9 | No regulatory body exists for sports education | Ministry to consult SAI on feasibility of such a regulator | Governance |
| 10 | Khelo India runs till March 2026 | Bring the scheme formally into SAI's organisational structure; have SAI (not IOA) organise the annual National Games; continue Fit India with dedicated staff | Governance/Operations |
| 11 | No mechanism exists to compare states' sporting performance | Create a **sports index** to rank states and drive competitiveness | Policy/Monitoring |

## Cross-check against what's already verified in this repo
`[INTERVIEW PREP]` Two of these land directly on top of NIPFP's independent findings (Module 4) — two separate, independent bodies (NIPFP field evaluation and a Parliamentary Committee) both flagging real operational gaps strengthens the credibility of using these as talking points:
- NIPFP found uneven KISCE manpower hiring (national average 56%); the Committee's ~45% SAI-wide vacancy figure is a related but distinct, more severe finding at the whole-organisation level — **do not conflate the two numbers**, they measure different things (KISCE-specific hiring vs. SAI-wide sanctioned-post vacancy).
- Both sources independently flag NCOE operational/completion gaps (NIPFP's infrastructure-completion data in Module 4 vs. the Committee's "some NCOEs non-operational" finding) — consistent, not contradictory.

## What NOT to do with this material
`[INTERVIEW PREP]` Do not volunteer the NSDF diversion finding as a talking point unprompted — it's a serious, specific integrity-adjacent finding (misuse of a development fund for civil-services associations/residential colonies) and repeating it casually in an interview could come across as either uninformed editorialising or, worse, implying knowledge of specific wrongdoing you can't substantiate beyond the Committee's own summary. If it comes up, the correct register is: "the Committee's report flagged a need for stricter regulation of NSDF's fund utilisation" — factual, not embellished.

## Problem → Administrative Response Matrix
`[INTERVIEW PREP]` Built from Modules 4, 6, and 7 combined — this is the single most useful document for scenario-question prep.

| Documented issue | Evidence source | Administrative domain | What an AD could realistically do | Sample interview question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~45% SAI-wide post vacancy; uneven KISCE hiring (avg. 56%) | Parliamentary Committee (2025) + NIPFP (2026) | HR/Operations | Analyse vacancy data by centre → escalate recruitment priority → monitor fill-rate against the Committee's 6-month benchmark | "How would you approach a chronic manpower shortage across field units?" |
| Make-in-India procurement constraint for specialised sports equipment | NIPFP | Finance/Procurement (GFR) | Identify the specification gap → document the absence of a compliant domestic product → route for conditional GFR flexibility per the evaluation's own recommendation | "A procurement is stuck because no domestic vendor meets a technical spec — what do you do?" |
| Infrastructure maintenance funding gap (grantees not self-funding upkeep) | NIPFP | Engineering/Finance | Flag at inspection → escalate to Finance/GAD → consider linking future disbursement to maintenance compliance | "You find a Khelo India facility falling into disrepair — what's your process?" |
| Centre-State coordination varies sharply (Rajasthan strong, others weak) | NIPFP | Coordination/Operations | Identify what the strong-coordination states are actually doing differently → propose replicable practice, not a generic directive | "How would you improve Centre-State coordination on infrastructure?" |
| NCOEs non-operational or lacking residential facilities; geographically disproportionate | Parliamentary Committee | Infrastructure/Operations | Distinguish "approved" from "operational" (as AR 2023-24 itself does: 24 approved, 22 operational) → report the gap precisely rather than the headline number | "SAI says it has 24 NCOEs — does that mean 24 are functioning?" |
| NSDF fund-utilisation concerns | Parliamentary Committee | Finance/Vigilance | Understand fund governance rules → recognise this as a compliance/vigilance-adjacent matter, not something to editorialise on | (Handle carefully — see note above) |
| No sports-education integration at NCOEs | Parliamentary Committee | Policy/Coordination | Recognise this as an inter-departmental (Education + Sports) coordination problem, consistent with Khelo Bharat Niti's Pillar 5 (Module 2) | "How should sports and education be better integrated at training centres?" |
