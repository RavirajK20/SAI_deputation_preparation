## 1. What is Khelo Bharat Niti-2025?

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The policy is formally titled **Khelo Bharat Niti-2025 (National Sports Policy-2025)**. It was launched by the Government on 01.07.2025.

The policy states that it is intended to harness sport for:
- sporting excellence;
- economic growth;
- social progress; and
- citizens' overall well-being.

It builds on earlier initiatives including the National Sports Policy-2001, Khelo India and TOPS, and seeks an inclusive sporting ecosystem from grassroots participation through the Olympic podium, including the Olympic ambition for 2036.

**Interview point:** Do not describe the policy merely as a "sports medal policy." Its stated conception is much broader: sport is treated as an instrument of national development.

## 2. Vision

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY, verified word-for-word across multiple independent sources]** The stated vision is:

> "Sports for Nation Building – Harness the Power of Sports for Nation's Holistic Development."

The policy connects four broad outcomes:
1. a stronger sporting culture and ecosystem;
2. international sporting excellence;
3. economic development through sport; and
4. social development, health and well-being through wider participation.

## 3. Union–State relationship

This is particularly important for a Government officer.

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The policy describes Union and State roles as complementary:

### Union Government
- national policy;
- financial and operational support;
- regulatory standards;
- advisory guidance;
- major-event facilitation; and
- national frameworks/direction.

### State Governments
- regional implementation;
- local sports infrastructure;
- grassroots programmes;
- additional financial support;
- monitoring local implementation; and
- response to regional needs.

**Administrative implication:** A sports programme can fail even when the Union-level policy is sound if State-level implementation, infrastructure, manpower, funding or monitoring is weak. An Assistant Director therefore needs coordination and monitoring skills, not only policy knowledge.

## 4. Twelve stated objectives

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The policy lists these objectives:

1. Comprehensive sports programmes from grassroots to elite levels.
2. Competitions and leagues at various levels.
3. Physical literacy and a culture of sports/physical activity.
4. Talent identification and development.
5. Equitable access to sports infrastructure.
6. Athlete-centric holistic support.
7. Sports science, medicine and innovation.
8. Stronger governance and institutional frameworks.
9. Revamped/sustainable funding mechanisms.
10. Economic growth through sports-related industries and activities.
11. Social development and inclusion through sport.
12. Sport as a viable career option for youth.

### Memory structure

Instead of memorising 12 disconnected bullets, group them:

**Participation → Competition → Talent → Infrastructure → Athlete support → Science → Governance → Finance → Economy → Inclusion → Careers.**

## 5. Five strategic pillars

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY/SAI implementation communication, independently cross-checked]** The five strategic pillars are:

1. **Excellence on the Global Stage**
2. **Sports for Economic Development**
3. **Sports for Social Inclusion and Empowerment**
4. **Sports as a People's Movement**
5. **Harmony with the National Education Policy 2020**

These should be understood as an integrated framework rather than five separate schemes.

### Pillar 1 — Excellence on the Global Stage

Think:
- grassroots-to-elite pathway;
- talent identification/development;
- competition structure;
- world-class infrastructure;
- coaching;
- sports science/medicine;
- technology; and
- stronger sports governance.

**Administrative question:** How do we convert a policy objective into measurable athlete-development outcomes?

### Pillar 2 — Sports for Economic Development

Think:
- sports industry;
- sports manufacturing;
- entrepreneurship/start-ups;
- sports tourism;
- major events;
- PPPs; and
- CSR/private investment.

**Administrative question:** How can public funding catalyse private participation without compromising transparency, value for money or public purpose?

### Pillar 3 — Social Inclusion and Empowerment

Think:
- women;
- rural/tribal communities;
- economically weaker sections;
- persons with disabilities;
- indigenous/traditional sports; and
- sport as a pathway for empowerment.

**Administrative question:** How should an administrator monitor whether an inclusion programme is actually reaching intended beneficiaries rather than merely meeting expenditure/output targets?

### Pillar 4 — Sports as a People's Movement

Think:
- mass participation;
- physical activity;
- fitness;
- community facilities;
- Fit India;
- healthier lifestyles.

**Administrative question:** How do we measure participation and behavioural change rather than simply counting events conducted?

### Pillar 5 — Harmony with NEP 2020

Think:
- integration of sport and education;
- physical literacy;
- school/college participation;
- education + sporting pathways;
- development of teachers/coaches and institutional capacity.

**Administrative question:** What coordination is needed between sports and education authorities?

## 6. What changed in the policy mindset?

**[INFERENCE grounded in the policy]** The important shift for interview purposes is not simply that India has a new policy document. The policy treats sport as a cross-sector development instrument.

The administrator therefore has to think across:

**Sports + Education + Health + Infrastructure + Finance + Industry + Inclusion + Technology + Governance.**

This explains why the Assistant Director vacancy's generic Government-administration duties are relevant inside SAI.

## 7. What does this mean for SAI?

**[OFFICIAL + INFERENCE]** SAI is one of the major institutions through which sports policy is operationalised. Its existing structure includes Operations, Khelo India, TOPS, Sports Science, IT, Engineering, Finance, Capacity Building, HR, Policy/Research & Innovation and other divisions.

Therefore Khelo Bharat Niti should be read as a **policy direction**, while SAI divisions translate parts of that direction into programmes, infrastructure, athlete support, monitoring, capacity building and institutional processes.

Do not say:
> "Khelo Bharat Niti tells SAI to do X, therefore SAI will automatically do X."

Better:
> "The policy establishes the national direction; implementation depends on the relevant schemes, institutional mandates, funding, rules, States, SAI and other stakeholders."

## 8. Administrative implications for an Assistant Director

The policy can generate exactly the kinds of work listed in the vacancy circular:

| Policy objective | Likely administrative work |
|---|---|
| Talent development | Scheme monitoring, data review, coordination, reports |
| Infrastructure | Procurement, project monitoring, utilisation, maintenance |
| Athlete support | Entitlements, contracts, service delivery, grievance handling |
| Sports science | Coordination, procurement, research/monitoring |
| Governance | Compliance, file examination, reporting, institutional reform |
| Economic development | PPP/CSR proposals, events, procurement, contracts |
| Inclusion | Beneficiary monitoring, State coordination, outcome assessment |
| Education integration | Inter-departmental coordination and programme monitoring |

## 9. Important related development: professional sports administration

**[OFFICIAL — RELATED DOCUMENT]** MYAS constituted a Task Force on Capacity Building of Sports Administrators under the Khelo Bharat NITI-2025 framework.

The Task Force identified, among other issues:
- lack of a professional cadre of sports administrators and weak institutional continuity;
- fragmented/outdated administrator training;
- gaps in sports-administration education;
- inadequate athlete pathways into governance; and
- limited use of digital tools and analytics in governance.

This is highly relevant to interview preparation because it shows that **sports administration itself is being treated as a capability that needs professionalisation**.

Do not make the stronger claim that this particular SAI vacancy was created because of the Task Force. No such causal link has been established.

## 11. Interview trap

**Question:** "Do you think the new Assistant Director posts were created because of Khelo Bharat Niti-2025?"

**Safe answer:**

> "I would not be able to establish that causal link from the vacancy circular alone. What I can say is that the policy is a major current framework for the sports sector, and the duties of the Assistant Director are directly relevant to the kind of implementation, coordination, monitoring and governance work required within SAI."

That is more defensible than inventing a reason for the vacancy.

## Source discipline

Do not convert policy aspirations into confirmed outcomes. Distinguish:

- **Policy objective** — what Government intends;
- **Scheme/programme** — how an objective is operationalised;
- **Implementation evidence** — what has actually happened;
- **Evaluation finding** — independently assessed performance;
- **Inference** — our analysis of what the policy means for the AD role.
