## 1. Founding and legal status
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]` SAI — successor organisation of the IXth Asian Games (New Delhi, 1982); established as a Society under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, per Resolution No. 1-1/83/SAI dated 25 January 1984, Department of Sports, Government of India. Confirmed word-for-word identical on both the SAI site and the AD vacancy circular's own "About SAI" section — two independent official sources agreeing.

## 4. Operational scheme structure `[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED — confirmed via SAI's own scheme page]`
SAI's Operation Division delivers its promotional schemes through:
- **10 Regional Centres**: Bengaluru, Kolkata, Gandhinagar, Kandivali (Mumbai), Bhopal, Sonepat, Lucknow, Chandigarh, Guwahati, Imphal
- **Academic wings**: NS NIS Patiala, LNCPE Thiruvananthapuram
- **Sports Science hubs concentrated at**: Patiala, Bengaluru, Kolkata (other centres upgrading toward this)

Three named schemes, confirmed directly:
1. **STC (SAI Training Centres)** — junior-level athletes, age group 10–18
2. **School scheme** — develops sports standards in schools/colleges with basic sports infrastructure
3. **NSTC (National Sports Talent Contest Scheme)** — scouts talent from schools, age group 8–14

`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]` Key statistics (from SAI's own site, previously confirmed): 9,000+ athletes, 1,000+ trainers/coaches, 180+ training centres, 12 Regional Centres, 4 promotional schemes, 20+ NCOEs.

## 5. The digital/service-delivery layer `[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]`
Schemes are delivered in part through `dbtyas-sports.gov.in` — a functioning DBT (Direct Benefit Transfer) platform with a Scheme Owner Login and a Sportsperson Login, handling Cash Incentive to medal winners, National Sports Awards, Pension to Meritorious Sportspersons, and HRDS. Confirmed directly by navigating the live portal.

**Interview implication:** don't pitch "introducing digital scheme delivery" — it already exists. The credible pitch is understanding/strengthening it (see Module 1).

## 6. Implementation reality — what NIPFP's Jan 2026 third-party evaluation actually found `[INDEPENDENT EVALUATION]`
This is real, MYAS-commissioned, field-verified (national + 5-state assessment, primary surveys) — independently confirmed by Claude by reading the report directly, not via secondary citation.

Concrete findings relevant to an AD's likely work:
- **Khelo India scale**: ~67% of Department of Sports' FY2025-26 budget (₹1,000 crore); 1,067 notified KICs (991 operational); 328 sanctioned infrastructure projects, 241 (74%) complete, 78 in progress, 9 at nil progress.
- **Manpower gaps are real and uneven**: national average only 56% of sanctioned KISCE posts filled (242 of 430); Bihar at just 12%, Chhattisgarh 40%, against states like Odisha/Arunachal Pradesh/Manipur at 100%.
- **NSRS portal**: reported glitches under heavy data load, and an awareness gap among users — confirmed as an actual documented finding, not an assumption.
- **Procurement constraint**: Make-in-India mandate can restrict sourcing of specialised sports equipment where no suitable domestic/BIS-certified product exists — report recommends conditional GFR flexibility for such cases.
- **Maintenance funding gap**: infrastructure grantees are supposed to self-fund maintenance; field visits found this frequently isn't happening, risking long-term asset sustainability.
- **Centre-State coordination varies sharply by state** — e.g., Rajasthan's strong SAI Regional Centre/State Sports Department coordination is explicitly credited for its high infrastructure completion count; weak coordination elsewhere is flagged as a bottleneck.
- Report recommends a dedicated **Project Management Unit (PMU)** at the central level and structured technical/administrative handholding for capacity-constrained states.

**Interview implication:** these are officially documented, evaluator-identified administrative problems — exactly the material for "here's a problem, what would you do" scenario questions. Use them as: *fact → documented gap → what an AD's role in addressing it would actually look like* (monitoring, coordination, data quality, escalation) — never as "SAI doesn't have X," since in most cases SAI has X and the gap is in execution/uptake, not absence.
