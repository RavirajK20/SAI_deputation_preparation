## 1. Governance — exact composition and timeline
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]`
- General Body: 36 members (including the President); reconstituted **16 November 2022**
- Governing Body: 28 members (including Chairman); reconstituted **1 September 2023**
- Both chaired by the Union Minister of Youth Affairs & Sports
- Member term: 3 years from nomination (except Ex-Officio members)
- During 2023-24, the Governing Body met twice: 58th meeting (19 Sept 2023), 59th meeting (20 Feb 2024)

`[INTERVIEW PREP]` If asked about SAI's governance structure, you can answer with actual dates and numbers instead of "there's a Governing Body" — materially stronger.

## 2. Organisational structure — an important nuance, not a contradiction
`[NOTE]` The 2023-24 Annual Report lists **19 divisions/institutions**, but the grouping differs somewhat from the live 2026 Divisions tab already verified in Module 1 (e.g., 2023-24 lists "Personnel Division" and "General Administration" separately with no standalone "HR," "IT," "Policy/Research & Innovation," "TAGG," or "Sports Science" divisions named the way the current site does). This most likely reflects genuine organisational restructuring between 2023-24 and 2026 — SAI evidently added/renamed divisions since this report — rather than an inconsistency in our research. Worth being aware of if a board member asks about SAI's structure: the org chart is not static.

2023-24 Divisions Coordination role specifically confirmed: **nodal division for liaising with MYAS and other agencies, particularly on Parliament and RTI matters** — useful concrete detail for "how does SAI handle Parliamentary accountability" questions.

## 3. Operational numbers, 2023-24 (verified, with an important precision the Key Statistics page glosses over)
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]`
- **NCOE**: 24 approved, but only **22 operational** in 2023-24 (Cooch Behar and Zirakpur not yet operational) — covering 13 priority disciplines (Archery, Athletics, Boxing, Cycling, Fencing, Hockey, Judo, Rowing, Swimming, Shooting, Table Tennis, Weightlifting, Wrestling) and 11 other disciplines. Sanctioned strength: 3,491 (1,800 residential, 1,691 non-residential); actual working strength: 3,159 athletes (1,657 boys, 1,502 girls).
- **STC**: 69 centres, 3,899 athletes (2,391 boys, 1,508 girls); age range 10–18 (individual events 12–18, team events 10–18); admission via specific competition-placement criteria (national/state championship rankings), not open enrolment
- **Extension Centres**: 26 centres, 426 athletes (273 boys, 153 girls); up to 20 athletes adopted per school/college
- **NSTC (all sub-schemes combined)**: 9 regular schools + 9 IGMA + 50 Akharas = 1,155 athletes (943 boys, 212 girls)

`[INTERVIEW PREP]` The NCOE "24 approved vs 22 operational" distinction is a good example of the kind of precision that separates a well-prepared answer from a memorised statistic — approved capacity and actual operational capacity are not the same thing, and a good administrator notices that gap.

## 4. Procurement — real numbers, genuinely useful for finance/procurement questions
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]` Equipment Support (ES) Division is SAI's central procurement division for sports goods/equipment/sports-science equipment across all Regional Centres and field units. **Total procurement 1 April 2023 – 31 March 2024: ₹38,75,06,477** (~₹38.75 crore). Real examples from the report: physiotherapy equipment (₹1.9 crore), sports kits for STC athletes (₹1.37 crore), a 2-year medical insurance rate contract for National Campers/Khelo India/NCOE/STC athletes (₹16.96 crore), sports shoes contracts with named vendors (Adidas India, Anandco Sporting), and even cricket kits supplied to the Cricket Association of Jamaica (international outreach, not just domestic).

`[INTERVIEW PREP]` This is real evidence that SAI procurement runs through standard GFR-style contract/tender/work-order mechanics at material scale — directly relevant if asked to discuss procurement judgement.

## 5. Coaching cadre — actual workforce composition
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]` As of 31.03.2024: **476 regular coaches, 308 contract coaches, 30 coaches on deputation** (814 total). During the year: 51 coaches retired, 4 voluntarily retired, 2 repatriated, 15 contract coaches resigned; 120 coaches received their 3rd MACP (Level-11 to Level-12); multiple promotion rounds (Assistant Coach→Coach, Senior Coach→Chief Coach, etc.).

`[INTERVIEW PREP]` This is a real HR/service-matter dataset — useful if a scenario question touches cadre management, MACP, or coach attrition.

## 6. Stadia — revenue generation is real, not incidental
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]` SAI's 5 Delhi stadia (JNS, IGSC, Dr. SPMSPC, MDCNS, Dr. KSSR) generated a combined **₹21,80,08,3243** (~₹21.8 crore) in 2023-24 across Come & Play fees, space licensing, sports/non-sports bookings, and hostel boarding. Stadia access was made free for sportspersons across the country from 1 November 2019 under the "Come & Play" scheme (launched May 2011), while non-sports use and space licensing remain fee-based revenue sources. 8,809 trainees registered under Come & Play in 2023-24 alone.

`[INTERVIEW PREP]` Good example of a public asset balancing free public-service access against revenue generation for maintenance — a genuinely interesting administrative tension to discuss if asked.

## 7. Regional Centre detail — Mumbai, cross-checked against Module 5
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED, consistent with Module 5]` Confirms independently: SAI RC Mumbai established 1989; RC Kandivali renamed SAI Shri Atal Bihari Vajpayee NCOE Mumbai per notification 13-07-2020; 37 acres; facilities include a synthetic athletics track, astro-turf hockey field, judo hall, boxing arena, squash court, and wrestling hall. Consistent across two independent sources now (SAI's RC Mumbai page and the Annual Report) — solid.

## 8. Compliance/governance activity — concrete, not abstract
`[OFFICIAL-VERIFIED]` The Rajbhasha (Official Language) Division underwent 8 separate Parliamentary Committee inspections across Regional Centres/STCs in 2023-24 alone, plus quarterly Official Language Implementation Committee meetings chaired by the Secretary, SAI. This is a genuine, recurring compliance/oversight cycle — useful concrete example if asked how SAI handles statutory/Parliamentary obligations beyond Khelo India specifically.
