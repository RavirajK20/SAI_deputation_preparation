Illustrative Example — Not a Documented Case

Start With a Person, Not a Chart

Before the boxes and arrows, here's the shape of the whole system through one invented, representative story. Every institution named below reappears — accurately — in the pages that follow.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE. Meera is fourteen, from a small town with no proper athletics track. A physical education teacher spots her sprint times at a National Sports Talent Contest (NSTC) trial. She's selected into a nearby SAI Training Centre (STC), where she trains for four years. At seventeen, her performance qualifies her for a National Centre of Excellence (NCOE), with better coaching and sports-science support. At nineteen, after a strong national meet, she's proposed for TOPS — the Mission Olympic Cell reviews her file and approves a customised support package, financed partly through the National Sports Development Fund.

Along the way: her STC's funding and staffing was monitored by SAI's Operations Division; her equipment came through General Administration & Procurement under GFR rules; a data record of her performances sits in NSRS; if her NSF's governance were ever in question, that would be a National Sports Board matter, not SAI's; and if Parliament ever reviewed whether her State's KISCE posts were adequately staffed, that finding would show up in a Parliamentary Standing Committee report, not a SAI press release.

An Assistant Director never coaches Meera. But an AD could be the person examining whether her STC's file properly recorded her progression, whether her NCOE's equipment procurement followed GFR, or whether her TOPS proposal's paperwork was in order before it went to the Mission Olympic Cell.
That's the whole handbook in one paragraph: policy → programme → institution → money → monitoring → an Assistant Director sitting inside the administrative machinery, not the athlete's story.

The Programme Layer Official — Current

The Talent Pipeline

Not one programme — a sequence, with different institutions responsible at each stage.

Precision worth remembering

SAI's Key Statistics page lists 24 NCOEs approved. The 2023-24 Annual Report shows only 22 were actually operational that year (Cooch Behar and Zirakpur were not yet running). Approved ≠ operational — a genuinely useful distinction in an interview.

Governance Layer Official — Current

Two Chains That Must Not Be Merged

The single most common conceptual error in this subject: treating NSGA's new bodies as if they sit inside, above, or in place of SAI. They don't. They're a parallel regulatory system.

SAI's operational chain

Function: implements sports development — trains athletes, runs centres, procures equipment, administers schemes.

NSGA-2025 regulatory chain

Function: governs and regulates National Sports Bodies — the Act's defined term covering the National Olympic Committee, National Paralympic Committee, NSFs, and Regional Sports Federations — sets standards, recognises/suspends, adjudicates disputes. Does not run training centres or athlete programmes.

Interview-safe line: "NSGA-2025 doesn't replace or absorb SAI. It's a separate statutory governance layer for what the Act calls National Sports Bodies — the Olympic and Paralympic Committees, NSFs, and Regional Sports Federations. The two chains only intersect where a body's governance status could affect its athletes' participation in SAI-linked programmes."

Digital Backbone Official — Current

The Systems Already Running

All confirmed directly against SAI's own IT Division description — nothing here is aspirational.

Athlete & scheme data

NSRS (National Sports Repository System) — SAI's central athlete/scheme data platform. Feeds data-analytics and AI/ML-based performance benchmarking and dashboards.

Public-facing

Khelo India website and mobile app, Fitness App, online facility booking for SAI stadia.

Internal administration

e-Office Premium, SPARROW (APAR system), file/leave/tour/personnel management modules.

Scheme delivery (Ministry-level)

dbtyas-sports.gov.in — a live DBT portal with Scheme Owner and Sportsperson logins, handling Cash Incentives, National Sports Awards, Pension to Meritorious Sportspersons, HRDS.

The AD angle: a technically fluent AD isn't introducing digital administration to SAI — SAI already runs it. The credible pitch is understanding and strengthening an existing, real system.

Study Utility Interview Prep

How to Read This Map in an Interview

Whatever scenario question you're asked, run it through these prompts in order before answering.

  1. Who makes policy on this? (MYAS / Department of Sports, or NSGA's NSB for governance standards)
  2. Who funds it? (Union budget, NSDF blend, or State co-funding)
  3. Who implements it? (SAI division, State Sports Department, or an NSF)
  4. Who monitors it? (The owning division, or Policy/Research & Innovation)
  5. Who regulates or recognises the body involved? (NSB, under NSGA — only for NSFs/sports bodies, not SAI's own schemes)
  6. Who audits or holds it accountable? (CAG, Parliament, Vigilance, RTI)
  7. Where would an AD actually intervene? (File examination, coordination, monitoring — rarely the technical/coaching decision itself)
  8. When would an AD escalate rather than decide? (Anything requiring higher financial sanction, policy interpretation, or cross-division authority)
