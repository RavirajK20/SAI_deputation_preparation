## 1. Why this Act matters

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The long title states that the Act provides for:
- development and promotion of sports;
- welfare measures for sportspersons;
- ethical practices based on good governance, ethics and fair play;
- alignment with Olympic/Paralympic principles, international best practices and legal standards; and
- unified, equitable and effective resolution of sports grievances and disputes.

This is therefore fundamentally a **governance and institutional framework**, not simply another sports-development scheme.

## 2. Act chronology — learn this exactly

### 18 August 2025
**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The President assented to the National Sports Governance Act, 2025 (No. 25 of 2025).

### 31 December 2025 notification
**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The Central Government notified a first set of provisions to come into force from **1 January 2026**.

The notification itself is important because Section 1(2) permits different provisions to commence on different dates.

### 12 January 2026
**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** National Sports Governance (National Sports Bodies) Rules, 2026 were notified.

### 8 January 2026
**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** National Sports Board (Search-cum-Selection Committee) Rules, 2026 were notified.

### 12 May 2026
**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** A second set of select provisions of the Act was notified by the Central Government. The Ministry hosts the notification separately on its NSGA page.

### 22 May 2026 / 26 May 2026 publication cycle
**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** National Sports Board Rules, 2026 and National Sports Tribunal Rules, 2026 were notified.

### June–August 2026
**[OFFICIAL — CURRENT IMPLEMENTATION]** The Ministry began/continued processes for selection of the National Sports Board Chairperson/Members and National Sports Tribunal Members, including extensions of application deadlines.

**Interview lesson:** Never answer simply "the Act came into force on 1 January 2026" without qualification. The Act uses **phased commencement**.

## 4. National Sports Bodies

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The Act creates a statutory governance framework for national sports governing bodies, including the National Olympic Committee, National Paralympic Committee, National Sports Federations and Regional Sports Federations.

The Act imposes governance/compliance requirements on National Sports Bodies.

The 2026 National Sports Bodies Rules provide operational detail concerning:
- sportspersons of outstanding merit;
- General Body and Executive Committee composition;
- elections;
- disqualifications;
- National Sports Election Panel;
- registration and updating of affiliate units.

## 5. Athlete/sportsperson representation

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY RULES/PIB]** The 2026 National Sports Bodies Rules require inclusion of at least four **Sportspersons of Outstanding Merit (SOMs)** in the General Body of National Sports Bodies, with specific provision for representation of women SOMs.

The rules use tiered eligibility criteria to identify qualifying sportspersons.

**Interview significance:** The governance model is not purely office-bearer-centric. Athlete/sportsperson participation is built into the statutory framework.

## 6. National Sports Board — what it is

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** Section 5 provides for establishment of the National Sports Board by the Central Government.

Section 6 gives the Board powers/functions including, among others:
- recognition of sports organisations;
- registration of affiliate units;
- suspension/cancellation of recognition or registration;
- maintenance of the register of National Sports Bodies and affiliates;
- inquiries;
- ad hoc administrative arrangements in specified circumstances;
- guidelines for Codes of Ethics;
- Safe Sports Policy;
- National Sports Election Panel;
- collaboration/guidance concerning international standards and best practices; and
- measures for protection of athlete/support-personnel rights and welfare.

The National Sports Board therefore occupies a **governance/recognition/compliance role**, not the same role as SAI's operational sports-development divisions.

## 7. National Sports Board — 2026 operationalisation

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** The National Sports Board Rules provide for the Board's composition, terms and conditions, and powers/functions.

The Ministry's June 2026 recruitment notice described the Board as the central authority for granting Board recognition to National Sports Bodies and ensuring compliance with governance, financial and ethical standards.

The Board consists of a **Chairperson and two Members**, appointed by the Central Government from a panel recommended by the Search-cum-Selection Committee.

**Search-cum-Selection Committee:** the 2026 rules provide a committee chaired by the Cabinet Secretary, with the Secretary (Sports), a person experienced in sports administration, and two recipients of national sports awards.

## 8. National Sports Tribunal

**[OFFICIAL — PRIMARY]** Chapter IX establishes the National Sports Tribunal and provides a statutory dispute-resolution mechanism.

The Act addresses:
- constitution of the Tribunal;
- removal of Chairperson/Members;
- Tribunal staff;
- jurisdiction;
- transfer of pending cases;
- bar on civil-court jurisdiction in specified matters;
- Tribunal powers;
- appeal to the Supreme Court; and
- execution of Tribunal orders.

**Administrative distinction:** The Tribunal is not SAI's internal grievance cell. It is a statutory adjudicatory mechanism created by Parliament.

## 9. Safe Sports and ethics

The Act includes a Code of Ethics and Safe Sports Policy framework.

The National Sports Board is empowered to frame Safe Sports Policy for protection and safety of women, minor athletes and other classes of persons.

**Interview significance:** Sports administration now includes institutional safeguards and athlete welfare as governance responsibilities, not merely performance management.

## 10. Recognised sports organisations: privileges and duties

The Act distinguishes between recognition and governance obligations.

A recognised sports organisation is not merely receiving Government support; recognition carries statutory governance expectations and duties.

This is important when thinking about:
- financial accountability;
- ethical conduct;
- elections;
- athlete welfare;
- compliance;
- governance standards; and
- continuing recognition.

## 11. What the 2026 Rules add

### National Sports Bodies Rules
**[OFFICIAL]** Operationalise governance standards around:
- SOM representation;
- General Body/Executive Committee;
- election procedures;
- disqualification;
- affiliate registration/update;
- National Sports Election Panel.

### National Sports Board Rules
**[OFFICIAL]** Operationalise:
- Board composition;
- terms of Chairperson/Members;
- service conditions;
- powers/functions;
- registers and related governance mechanisms.

### National Sports Tribunal Rules
**[OFFICIAL]** Operationalise the Tribunal's institutional/service framework.

### Search-cum-Selection Committee Rules
**[OFFICIAL]** Establish the mechanism for recommending Chairperson and Members of the National Sports Board.

## 12. What this does NOT mean

Do not say:

> "The NSGA-2025 has replaced SAI."

It has not.

Do not say:

> "The National Sports Board will run SAI's schemes."

That is not the role described by the Act.

Do not say:

> "The Act completely came into force on 1 January 2026."

The commencement is phased.

Do not say:

> "Every current sports dispute goes directly to the Tribunal."

The Act specifies the Tribunal's jurisdiction and exclusions; those provisions must be read before making a case-specific statement.

## 13. Authority-vs-function map

| Function | Primary institutional location |
|---|---|
| National sports policy | MYAS / Department of Sports |
| SAI schemes/operations | SAI and relevant partners |
| Recognition/governance of National Sports Bodies | National Sports Board under NSGA framework |
| Sports-body elections | Sports-body framework + National Sports Election Panel mechanisms |
| Ethics/Safe Sports governance | NSGA framework / recognised sports organisations / Board functions |
| Sports disputes within Tribunal jurisdiction | National Sports Tribunal |
| Anti-doping | NADA/NDTL and applicable anti-doping framework |

**Caution:** This table is a study simplification. Actual authority depends on the statute, rules, scheme guidelines and the subject matter of the particular case.

## 14. Why an Assistant Director in SAI should know this

The vacancy duties include compliance/governance, legal work, inter-departmental coordination, file examination, monitoring and representation.

Therefore an AD should know when an issue is:

**an SAI operational matter**
vs.
**a Ministry policy matter**
vs.
**a National Sports Board governance matter**
vs.
**a Tribunal/dispute matter**
vs.
**an NSF/internal sports-body matter**.

That jurisdictional instinct is more valuable than memorising isolated provisions.

## Source discipline

For interview answers, distinguish four dates/events:

**18 Aug 2025:** Act enacted.

**1 Jan 2026:** first selected provisions commenced.

**12 Jan 2026:** National Sports Bodies Rules notified.

**May–June 2026:** further provisions/rules and institutional selection processes advanced.

Do not collapse these into a single "commencement date".
