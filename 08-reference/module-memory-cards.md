# Module Memory Cards — 15-Minute Review

## Module 1 — Governance Architecture
**Core idea:** MYAS sets policy/funds; SAI implements; States co-implement; NSFs govern sport-specific competition; new NSGA bodies (NSB/NST) regulate/adjudicate.
**Three facts:** SAI has 19 divisions; SAI's IT Division runs NSRS + AI/ML "sports intelligence"; NSDF blends Government + CSR funding.
**One number/date:** 25.01.1984 — SAI's founding resolution.
**One documented problem:** N/A at this module's level (see Modules 4/7 for implementation problems).
**Why an AD should care:** The "authority vs function" test (who owns policy/money/execution/monitoring/decision) resolves most scenario questions correctly.

## Module 2 — Khelo Bharat Niti-2025
**Core idea:** Sport as a nation-building instrument across 5 pillars, not just a medals policy.
**Three facts:** Vision = "Sports for Nation Building"; 12 objectives; Union sets policy/standards, States implement locally.
**One number/date:** Launched 01.07.2025.
**One documented problem:** Task Force on Capacity Building of Sports Administrators flagged lack of a professional administrator cadre.
**Why an AD should care:** Explains why a Government-administration generalist is wanted for this post, not a sports specialist.

## Module 3 — NSGA-2025
**Core idea:** New statutory governance/regulatory framework for sports bodies, phased into force.
**Three facts:** Enacted 18.08.2025; created National Sports Board + National Sports Tribunal; commencement happened in stages, not all at once.
**One number/date:** 31.12.2025 (first notification), 12.05.2026 (second).
**One documented problem:** N/A — too new for implementation evaluation yet.
**Why an AD should care:** NSF governance disputes now route through NSB/NST, not SAI — know who owns what.

## Module 4 — SAI Organisation/Machinery
**Core idea:** SAI is a major autonomous institution with its own finance, schemes, and governance — not a passive execution arm.
**Three facts:** 19 divisions; DBT portal already exists for scheme delivery; NIPFP independently evaluated implementation in Jan 2026.
**One number/date:** 56% average KISCE manpower fill rate (NIPFP).
**One documented problem:** NSRS load/glitch issues; Make-in-India procurement constraints for specialised equipment.
**Why an AD should care:** Don't pitch "introducing" digital systems that already exist — pitch understanding/strengthening them.

## Module 5 — NCOE/STC/TOPS
**Core idea:** A talent pipeline, not one programme — NSTC (scout) → STC (develop) → NCOE (specialise) → TOPS (elite medal support).
**Three facts:** NCOE origin traces to an 18.09.2019 OM; TOPS's MOC handles proposal→sanction→review; SABV NCOE Mumbai is the renamed Kandivali centre.
**One number/date:** 24 approved NCOEs.
**One documented problem:** "MOC meets weekly" is unconfirmed — do not state it.
**Why an AD should care:** Even elite athlete support runs on an administrative cycle (proposal→sanction→monitoring) — directly maps to AD duties.

## Module 6 — SAI Annual Report 2023-24
**Core idea:** What SAI actually did in a real year, with numbers.
**Three facts:** ₹38.75cr procurement in FY23-24; 814 total coaches; ₹21.8cr stadia revenue.
**One number/date:** 24 approved NCOEs, but only 22 operational.
**One documented problem:** No SAI Annual Report 2024-25 could be located — 2023-24 is the latest verified.
**Why an AD should care:** Shows real administrative cycles (procurement, HR, revenue) at material scale — this is the job's actual texture.

## Module 7 — 369th Parliamentary Committee
**Core idea:** Independent legislative oversight, separate from SAI's or NIPFP's own accounts.
**Three facts:** Recommended converting SAI to a statutory body with its own cadre; ~45% SAI-wide post vacancy; recommended SAI (not IOA) run the National Games.
**One number/date:** Report presented 20.08.2025.
**One documented problem:** NSDF fund diversion to civil-services associations/residential colonies — handle this fact carefully, don't editorialise.
**Why an AD should care:** This is the "Problem → Administrative Response" material — the most likely source of realistic scenario questions.
